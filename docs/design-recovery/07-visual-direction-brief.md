# Visual direction brief

Date: 3 August 2026  
Owners: Product Design + Impeccable `shape`  
Status: input to three independent rendered concepts

## Shared product truth

- Phone canvas: 390 x 844 CSS pixels, light mode, no device frame or browser chrome.
- Product: Levodopa Day Map, a calm local-first schedule explainer.
- User: a person in their mid-60s with Parkinson's; likely fatigue, tremor, and cognitive load.
- Reference time: 09:30.
- Next dose: 11:30, 100 mg, half tablet, in 2 hrs.
- Later doses: 15:00 and 19:00, both 100 mg, half tablet.
- Earlier dose anchor: 07:30, 200 mg, 1 tablet.
- Model statement: `Schedule model` / `Rising after the 07:30 dose`.
- Boundary: `Modeled from this schedule—not symptoms or measured medication levels.`
- Global destinations: My Day and Analyze. Schedule is contextual.

## Shared craft constraints

- Calm, warm, precise, and adult; never childish, gamified, futuristic, or institutional.
- Fewer containers and restrained radii. Alignment, time, whitespace, and dividers do most grouping work.
- Strong but not enormous type. A mid-60s user should read it easily without turning every label into a poster.
- Maintained, familiar icons only. Do not invent clinical symbols or reuse the incumbent abstract forecast glyphs.
- No generic dashboard cards, glassmorphism, gradients behind medical copy, glowing shadows, or ornamental graphs.
- One clear focal point: the next dose.
- A compact day orientation must fit before the fold and remain understandable without color.
- The model statement is visually supportive and clearly qualified.
- Bottom navigation, if used, reserves space and never overlaps content.

## Direction A: temporal itinerary

Promote the vertical itinerary mechanism. Use a quiet editorial composition with a slim chronological spine, an unmistakable next-dose stop, compact later rows, and the day orientation woven into the timeline. The emotional register is warm confidence: considered, human, and private. Avoid card stacks; allow one gently bounded next-dose region at most.

## Direction B: daylight bands

Promote the horizontal day-band mechanism. The next dose remains first, followed by a clear Morning / Afternoon / Evening / Overnight orientation with dose anchors and a current-time marker. Use a modern, calm visual language that feels closer to a well-designed weather or transit guide than a health dashboard. Keep the model statement as a small explanatory strip.

## Direction C: medication ledger

Promote the dose-ledger mechanism. Make the medicine schedule feel tangible and orderly: strong time alignment, tablet identity, compact rows, and a restrained model-context inset. The register is refined and trustworthy without becoming hospital software. The hierarchy should make cross-dose comparison effortless while keeping 11:30 dominant.

## Comparison test

The three renders pass only if a reviewer can identify meaningful differences in information framing and layout with the color removed. Palette or font changes alone do not count as distinct directions.
